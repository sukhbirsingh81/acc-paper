\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a service-oriented 4D path-planning architecture for structured corridor operations within U-space. Topology, A* corridor routing, waypoint synthesis, CFMS trajectory generation, NDMap deconfliction and the interface towards the USSP are separate service responsibilities. The Planning Service computes routes and trajectories and changes the request-local graph for re-routing, and the Deconfliction Service detects and reports conflicts with NDMap. The USSP comes after these services: the planner prepares a candidate trajectory, but it does not authorize flights.

The evaluation on the Hamburg vertiport network gave three main results, one per research question. First (RQ1), with deconfliction disabled, A* search and waypoint synthesis stayed below one millisecond, while CFMS invocation and dense trajectory generation took almost all of the planning time. Over the \paPairs{} tested routes, the dependence on route length is explained by the almost linear growth of the number of trajectory samples with route length. Second (RQ2), with conflict checking enabled, all \cfRequests{} requests over the full day ended with an accepted, locally deconflicted candidate plan with no detected conflict, \cfResolved{} of them after lateral re-routing. Shorter scheduling windows of 60, 30 and 15 minutes led to more overlap and more detected conflicts in this fixed-seed run and showed retry-limit and no-route outcomes, and the scripted encounters confirmed which situations lateral re-routing can and cannot solve. A conflict is only found when both trajectories are too close at the same moment, so overlapping flight times are only a precondition for checking a pair. The detector does not guarantee to find every conflict in continuous time, because it checks sampled times and does not report violations shorter than \SI{1}{s}. Third (RQ3), the latency breakdown showed that the conflict-detection stage, which in the current stateless workflow reloads all peer trajectories and rebuilds the detector state for every check, dominates request time once many peers are stored, exceeding the planning time itself.

These results characterize a sequential single-machine prototype, not operational capacity, and the density results come from one fixed OD sequence and seed. Future work should look at larger networks, many runs with different seeds, concurrent requests, a distributed deployment with a shared trajectory store, a detector state that is kept between checks, resolution in time and altitude, and more realistic constraints such as terrain, weather, obstacles and dynamic traffic.
