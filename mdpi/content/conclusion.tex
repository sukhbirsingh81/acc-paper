\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we presented a service-oriented 4D path-planning architecture for structured corridor operations within U-space. Topology, A* corridor routing, waypoint synthesis, CFMS trajectory generation, NDMap deconfliction and the interface towards the USSP are separate service responsibilities. The Planning Service computes routes and trajectories and changes the request-local graph for re-routing, and the Deconfliction Service detects and reports conflicts with NDMap. The USSP comes after these services: the planner prepares a candidate trajectory, but it does not authorize flights.

The evaluation on the Hamburg vertiport network gave three main results, one per research question. First (RQ1), with deconfliction switched off, A* search and waypoint synthesis stayed below one millisecond, while CFMS invocation and dense trajectory generation took almost all of the planning time. Over the \paPairs{} tested routes, the dependence on route length is explained by the almost linear growth of the number of trajectory samples with route length. Second (RQ2), with conflict checking switched on, all \cfRequests{} requests over the full day ended with an accepted, locally deconflicted plan, \cfResolved{} of them after lateral re-routing. Shorter scheduling windows of 60, 30 and 15 minutes led to more overlap and more conflicts in this fixed-seed run and showed retry-limit and no-route outcomes, and the scripted encounters confirmed which situations lateral re-routing can and cannot solve. A conflict is only found when both trajectories are too close at the same moment, so overlapping flight times are only a precondition for checking a pair. The detector does not guarantee to find every conflict in continuous time, because it checks sampled times and does not report violations shorter than \SI{1}{s}. Third (RQ3), the latency breakdown showed that conflict detection, which loads all peer trajectories and rebuilds the detector state for every check, takes most of the request time once many peers are stored, more than planning itself.

These results describe a sequential prototype on a single machine, not operational capacity, and they come from one traffic sample: the density runs use one fixed OD sequence and seed, so the conflict shares describe this run and not traffic in general. Future work should look at larger networks, many runs with different seeds, concurrent requests, a distributed deployment with a shared trajectory store, a detector state that is kept between checks, resolution in time and altitude, and more realistic constraints such as terrain, weather, obstacles and dynamic traffic.
