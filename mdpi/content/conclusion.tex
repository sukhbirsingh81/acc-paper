\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a service-oriented 4D path-planning architecture for structured corridor operations within U-space. The architecture separates topology provision, A*-based corridor routing, waypoint synthesis, CFMS-based trajectory generation, NDMap-based deconfliction, and a USSP-facing authorization boundary into distinct service responsibilities. The Planning Service owns route computation, trajectory generation, and request-local graph modification for lateral re-routing, while the Deconfliction Service owns NDMap conflict detection and conflict reporting. The USSP-facing boundary is downstream of these services: the planner prepares candidate 4D intent, but does not authorize U-space operations.

The Hamburg vertiport corridor-network evaluation produced three main results, one per research question. First (RQ1), in the planner-stage latency decomposition with deconfliction disabled, A* path computation and waypoint synthesis remained below one millisecond, while CFMS invocation and dense trajectory generation dominated planner-stage runtime; over the \paPairs{} evaluated route geometries, the route-length dependence on the fixed 29-node network is explained by the near-linear growth of the trajectory sample count with route length. Second (RQ2), in the conflict-resolution-enabled experiment, all \cfRequests{} sequential full-day requests were retained as locally deconflicted candidate plans, with \cfResolved{} conflicts resolved by lateral re-routing; compressing the scheduling window to 60, 30, and 15 minutes increased temporal overlap and conflict frequency in that fixed-seed realization and exposed retry-limit and no-route outcomes, and a deterministic encounter suite confirmed which geometries lateral re-routing can and cannot resolve. Conflicts are established co-temporally---both trajectories are evaluated at one common instant---so overlapping flight windows are only a precondition for checking a pair. The evaluated implementation does not provide a formal continuous-time completeness guarantee, because the predicate is evaluated at sampled instants and violation intervals shorter than \SI{1}{s} are not reported. Third (RQ3), the end-to-end latency decomposition showed that per-check conflict detection---which re-fetches all peer trajectories and rebuilds detector state---dominates request time once peers accumulate, exceeding the planning cost itself.

These results characterize a single-machine sequential prototype, not operational capacity, and they rest on one traffic realization: the density scenarios use a single fixed OD sequence and schedule-time seed, so the reported conflict fractions describe that realization rather than a traffic population. Future work should evaluate larger networks and network-size scaling, replicated traffic samples over many seeds, concurrent request loads, distributed deployment with a shared trajectory store, persistent or incrementally updated detector state, temporal and vertical deconfliction, and more realistic constraints including terrain, weather, obstacle avoidance, and dynamic traffic.
