\Title{Service-Oriented 4D Planning for Structured Corridor Operations within U-space: Latency and Lateral Re-Routing}

\Author{Sukhbir Singh $^{1,2,}$* and Enno Nagel $^{2}$}
\AuthorNames{Sukhbir Singh and Enno Nagel}

\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Technical University of Munich (TUM), Germany\\
$^{2}$ \quad DLR Institute of Flight Guidance, Germany}

\corres{Correspondence: sukhbir.singh@dlr.de}

\abstract{Urban air mobility requires efficient trajectory planning and conflict checking. We present a research prototype for structured corridor operations within U-space, preparing candidate flight plans before authorization. A Planning Service searches a corridor graph, constructs flight-phase waypoints and generates a four-dimensional trajectory; a separate Deconfliction Service checks it against previously retained plans. Detected conflicts trigger lateral re-routing. We evaluate the prototype sequentially on one machine using an exemplary network of 29 Hamburg vertiports. With conflict checking disabled, route search and waypoint construction each remain below one millisecond across 100 origin--destination pairs measured three times. Trajectory generation accounts for almost all planning time and increases with route length. With conflict checking enabled, all 100 requests scheduled across one day produce retained candidate plans, three after re-routing. Compressing the scheduling window to 60, 30 and 15 minutes increases detected conflicts and produces failures of the re-routing strategy. Conflict-checking latency exceeds planning latency as the peer set grows; each check reloads trajectories and rebuilds detector state. These results characterize one fixed request sequence, not operational capacity or population conflict probabilities. Retention means no conflict was detected under the sampled criterion; it does not establish continuous-time separation.}

\keyword{urban air mobility; U-space; four-dimensional trajectory planning; path planning; deconfliction; conflict detection; microservices; A* search; vertiport corridor network}

\renewcommand{\addhighlights}{%
\mbox{}\\[2pt]
\noindent\textbf{What are the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item A prototype for structured corridor operations within U-space combines route search, waypoint synthesis and trajectory generation in a Planning Service, with conflict detection in a separate Deconfliction Service.
\item On the Hamburg corridor network, trajectory generation with the flight-management system takes almost all of the planning time, while graph search and waypoint synthesis stay below one millisecond.
\end{itemize}\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{What are the implications of the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item A new lateral route can be found quickly, but regenerating the trajectory and, above all, the current stateless conflict check against a growing number of stored trajectories are the main costs of re-planning.
\item The results are a local, sequential baseline for a prototype and not a capacity assessment.
\end{itemize}}
