\Title{Service-Oriented 4D Path Planning for Structured Corridor Operations within U-space: Planner-Stage Latency Decomposition and Conflict-Driven Lateral Re-Routing}

\abstract{Urban air mobility requires efficient path-planning and conflict-management services for structured low-altitude operations. This paper presents a service-oriented four-dimensional path-planning framework for preparing candidate operation intent within U-space. The framework combines graph-based shortest-path routing, flight-management-based trajectory generation, multi-dimensional conflict checking, and request-local lateral re-routing. Its architecture separates topology provision, route planning, waypoint synthesis, trajectory generation, deconfliction, and a U-space Service Provider-facing authorization boundary into distinct responsibilities. The prototype is evaluated on a fixed 29-node Hamburg vertiport corridor network using sequential local experiments. In a planner-stage latency-decomposition experiment with deconfliction disabled, covering 100 origin--destination pairs measured three times each, path search and waypoint synthesis remain sub-millisecond, while invocation of the flight-management engine and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and explain its dependence on selected route length. In a conflict-resolution-enabled experiment with 100 sequential operator requests over one scheduling day, all 100 candidate trajectories are retained as locally deconflicted plans: 96 are conflict-free on the first deconfliction check, one has no peer trajectory, and three are resolved by lateral re-routing. Pairwise temporal overlap is sparse in this baseline---118 of 4950 candidate--peer pairs overlap---while 59 of the 99 requests with a non-empty peer set have at least one overlapping peer, and actual conflicts remain rare. Narrower 15, 30, and 60 minute scheduling windows produce more conflicts and expose retry-limit and no-route outcomes in which lateral alternatives are exhausted; these density scenarios use one fixed origin--destination sequence and schedule-time seed, so they characterize the implemented loop rather than estimate population-level conflict probabilities. A latency decomposition shows that per-check conflict detection, which re-fetches all peer trajectories and rebuilds the detector state, dominates end-to-end request time once peers accumulate. The results are a single-machine sequential baseline for candidate trajectory preparation before operational authorization by a U-space Service Provider.}

\mbox{}\\[2pt]
\noindent\textbf{What are the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item A service-oriented U-space planning prototype separates route planning, waypoint synthesis, four-dimensional trajectory generation, and multi-dimensional conflict detection.
\item In the evaluated Hamburg corridor benchmark, invocation of the flight-management engine and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and its route-length dependence, while graph-based path search and waypoint synthesis remain below one millisecond.
\end{itemize}\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{What are the implications of the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item Lateral route alternatives can be searched quickly, but repeated trajectory generation and, above all, per-check conflict detection against a growing peer set are the main costs of conflict-driven re-planning.
\item The evaluated results are a local sequential baseline for a prototype, not an operational capacity assessment.
\end{itemize}}
