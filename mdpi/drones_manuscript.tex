\Title{Service-Oriented 4D Path Planning for Structured Corridor Operations within U-space: Planner-Stage Latency Decomposition and Conflict-Driven Lateral Re-Routing}

\Author{Sukhbir Singh $^{1,2,}$* and Enno Nagel $^{2}$}
\AuthorNames{Sukhbir Singh and Enno Nagel}

\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Technical University of Munich (TUM), Germany\\
$^{2}$ \quad DLR Institute of Flight Guidance, Germany}

\corres{Correspondence: sukhbir.singh@dlr.de}

\abstract{Structured low-altitude urban air mobility needs efficient path-planning and conflict-management services. This paper presents an initial implementation of a service-oriented four-dimensional path-planning framework that prepares candidate operation intent within U-space. It separates topology provision, route planning, waypoint synthesis, flight-management-based trajectory generation, conflict checking, and a U-space Service Provider-facing authorization boundary into distinct services, and resolves conflicts by request-local lateral re-routing. The prototype is evaluated on a fixed 29-node Hamburg vertiport corridor network. With deconfliction disabled, across 100 origin--destination pairs, path search and waypoint synthesis remain sub-millisecond, while flight-management-engine invocation and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and its dependence on route length. With conflict checking enabled, all 100 sequential requests over one scheduling day are retained as locally deconflicted plans, three after lateral re-routing. Compressing the scheduling window to 60, 30, and 15 minutes produces more conflicts and exposes retry-limit and no-route outcomes where lateral alternatives run out; these use one fixed request sequence and seed, so they characterize the loop rather than estimate conflict probabilities. Per-check conflict detection, which re-fetches all peer trajectories and rebuilds detector state, dominates end-to-end request time once peers accumulate. The results are a single-machine sequential baseline for candidate trajectory preparation before U-space authorization.}

\keyword{urban air mobility; U-space; four-dimensional trajectory planning; path planning; deconfliction; conflict detection; microservices; A* search; vertiport corridor network}

\renewcommand{\addhighlights}{%
\mbox{}\\[2pt]
\noindent\textbf{What are the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item A service-oriented U-space planning prototype separates route planning, waypoint synthesis, four-dimensional trajectory generation, and multi-dimensional conflict detection.
\item In the evaluated Hamburg corridor benchmark, invocation of the flight-management engine and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and its route-length dependence, while graph-based path search and waypoint synthesis remain below one millisecond.
\end{itemize}\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{What are the implications of the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item Lateral route alternatives can be searched quickly, but repeated trajectory generation and, above all, per-check conflict detection against a growing peer set are the main costs of conflict-driven re-planning.
\item The evaluated results are a local sequential baseline for a prototype, not an operational capacity assessment.
\end{itemize}}
