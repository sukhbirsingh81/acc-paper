\Title{Service-Oriented 4D Path Planning for Structured Corridor Operations within U-space: Planner-Stage Latency Decomposition and Conflict-Driven Lateral Re-Routing}

\Author{Sukhbir Singh $^{1,2,}$*, Enno Nagel $^{2}$ and Bianca Isabella Schuchardt $^{2}$}
\AuthorNames{Sukhbir Singh, Enno Nagel and Bianca Isabella Schuchardt}

\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Technical University of Munich (TUM), Germany\\
$^{2}$ \quad DLR Institute of Flight Guidance, Germany}

\corres{Correspondence: please add corresponding author email address}

\abstract{Urban air mobility requires efficient path-planning and conflict-management services for structured low-altitude operations. This paper presents a service-oriented four-dimensional path-planning framework for preparing candidate operation intent within U-space. The framework combines graph-based shortest-path routing, flight-management-based trajectory generation, multi-dimensional conflict checking, and request-local lateral re-routing. Its architecture separates topology provision, route planning, waypoint synthesis, trajectory generation, deconfliction, and a U-space Service Provider-facing authorization boundary into distinct responsibilities. The prototype is evaluated on a fixed 29-node Hamburg vertiport corridor network using sequential local experiments. In a planner-stage latency-decomposition experiment with deconfliction disabled, path search and waypoint synthesis remain sub-millisecond, while invocation of the flight-management engine and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and explain its dependence on selected route length. In a conflict-resolution-enabled experiment with 100 sequential operator requests over one scheduling day, all 100 candidate trajectories are retained as locally deconflicted plans: 96 are conflict-free on the first deconfliction check, one has no peer trajectory, and three are resolved by lateral re-routing; narrower 15, 30, and 60 minute scheduling windows produce proportionally more conflicts and expose retry-limit and no-route outcomes in which lateral alternatives are exhausted. A latency decomposition shows that per-check conflict detection, which re-fetches all peer trajectories and rebuilds the detector state, dominates end-to-end request time once peers accumulate. The results are a single-machine sequential baseline for candidate trajectory preparation before operational authorization by a U-space Service Provider.}

\keyword{urban air mobility; U-space; four-dimensional trajectory planning; path planning; deconfliction; conflict detection; microservices; A* search; vertiport corridor network}

\mbox{}\\[2pt]
\noindent\textbf{What are the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item A service-oriented U-space planning prototype separates route planning, waypoint synthesis, four-dimensional trajectory generation, and multi-dimensional conflict detection.
\item In the evaluated Hamburg corridor benchmark, invocation of the flight-management engine and dense trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and its route-length dependence, while graph-based path search and waypoint synthesis remain below one millisecond.
\end{itemize}\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{What are the implications of the main findings?}
\begin{itemize}[labelsep=2.5mm,topsep=-3pt]
\item Lateral route alternatives can be searched quickly, but repeated trajectory generation and, above all, per-check conflict detection against a growing peer set are the main costs of conflict-driven re-planning.
\item The evaluated results are a local sequential baseline for a prototype, not an operational capacity assessment.
\end{itemize}}
