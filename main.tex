\begin{abstract}
Urban Air Mobility (UAM) requires scalable path-planning and conflict-management services for structured low-altitude corridor operations. This paper presents a service-oriented 4D path-planning framework for U-space corridor operations that combines A*-based routing, CFMS-based time-parameterized trajectory generation, and NDMap-based conflict checking with request-local lateral re-routing. The architecture separates topology provision, route planning, waypoint synthesis, trajectory generation, deconfliction, and U-space Service Provider (USSP) interaction into distinct service responsibilities. The prototype is evaluated on a Hamburg vertiport corridor network using two local, sequential experiments. First, a planner-stage scaling experiment with deconfliction disabled decomposes runtime into A* routing, waypoint synthesis, CFMS invocation and trajectory generation, and the planner-stage subtotal. A* path search and waypoint synthesis remain sub-millisecond in the evaluated benchmark, while CFMS invocation and trajectory generation dominate planner-stage runtime and determine the observed scaling with selected path distance. Second, a conflict-resolution-enabled experiment uses NDMap conflict checking against previously accepted trajectories and lateral re-routing by request-local edge removal. In 100 sequential requests, 97 requests were accepted: 93 were conflict-free on the first deconfliction check, one was the first request with no peer trajectory available, and three were successfully resolved by lateral re-routing. Three requests failed, consisting of one retry-limit case and two routing/input-graph errors. The results should be interpreted as a local, sequential baseline evaluation of a service-oriented prototype, not as an operational capacity assessment.
\end{abstract}
