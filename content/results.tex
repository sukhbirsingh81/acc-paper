\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{BLAN--WOHL Trajectory Case}
\label{sec:reference_trajectory}
\label{sec:blan_wohl_trajectory}

To illustrate the route and trajectory-generation result produced by
\texttt{acc-trajcalc}, we use the request with take-off vertiport \texttt{BLAN}
and landing vertiport \texttt{WOHL}. The request uses the fixed cruise layer of
\SI{200}{m} MSL and does not invoke \texttt{acc-trajdeconf}; deconfliction is
treated as a separate service boundary in this architecture. For this
origin--destination pair, the A* planner selects the
corridor sequence
\[
\texttt{BLAN} \rightarrow \texttt{VOLK} \rightarrow \texttt{SCHN} \rightarrow
\texttt{FUHB} \rightarrow \texttt{WOHL}.
\]

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_paths_overview} shows this route in the context of
the Hamburg corridor graph. The thinner blue edges represent available
corridor connections, the red markers show graph nodes, and the thicker blue
polyline highlights the selected A* path from \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL}.
This overview makes the global routing result visible before the route is
expanded into a detailed 4D trajectory.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_paths_overview.png}
  \caption{A* corridor path overview for the BLAN to WOHL
  request. The highlighted path connects the departure vertiport
  BLAN with the landing vertiport WOHL through the selected
  corridor sequence.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_paths_overview}
\end{figure}

The waypoint synthesizer expands this five-node route into 12 CFMS input
waypoints, including departure, transition, cruise, top-of-descent, and landing
waypoints. The resulting dense trajectory contains 1646 samples, covers a
cumulative distance of \SI{29.13}{km}, and has a total duration of
\SI{1413.61}{s}, or \SI{23.56}{min}. The mean true airspeed over the sampled
trajectory is \SI{19.81}{m/s}, with the cruise segment reaching approximately
\SI{24.93}{m/s}.

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_map} separates the lateral trajectory by the main
planning phases. The departure panel shows the vehicle leaving \texttt{BLAN},
climbing through the terminal waypoints, and joining the corridor at
\texttt{VOLK}. The cruise panel shows the route through the selected corridor
nodes \texttt{VOLK}, \texttt{SCHN}, and \texttt{FUHB}. The landing panel then
shows the inbound transition from \texttt{FUHB} toward the destination
vertiport \texttt{WOHL}. The figure therefore confirms that the generated
trajectory follows the A*-selected corridor sequence while adding the terminal
geometry required at both endpoints.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_trajectory_map.png}
  \caption{Phase-separated lateral trajectory for the BLAN to
  WOHL request. Red markers indicate synthesized CFMS waypoints; the
  blue line is the generated trajectory. The panels separate the take-off and
  ascent portion, the corridor cruise portion, and the final landing transition.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_map}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_altitude} shows the vertical profile in a smaller
standalone format. During take-off and ascent, the trajectory climbs from the
pad through the terminal heights and reaches the first corridor node below the
cruise layer. The vehicle then reaches and maintains the \SI{200}{m} MSL cruise
layer during the central corridor segment. On arrival, the trajectory holds the
cruise layer until the inbound descent segment, then descends through the
\SI{60}{m} and \SI{15}{m} terminal references before reaching the \texttt{WOHL}
pad. This profile illustrates how the waypoint-synthesis logic combines the
fixed cruise altitude with structured vertiport departure and arrival
procedures.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_trajectory_altitude.png}
  \caption{Altitude profile for the BLAN to WOHL trajectory.
  The three panels show take-off/ascent, cruise, and landing using local
  phase-relative time axes. The profile reaches the fixed \SI{200}{m} MSL
  cruise layer before descending into WOHL.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_altitude}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_tas} shows the corresponding true airspeed profile.
The vehicle accelerates from the terminal take-off segment into the outbound
transition, reaches the higher cruise setting along the central corridor
segment, and then decelerates for the arrival and landing procedure.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering

  \caption{True airspeed profile for the BLAN to WOHL
  trajectory. The phase-separated panels show the acceleration from take-off,
  the higher cruise-speed segment, and the deceleration into the landing
  procedure.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_tas}
