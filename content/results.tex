\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{BLAN--WOHL Trajectory Case}
\label{sec:blan_wohl_trajectory}

This section first presents one representative trajectory case to illustrate the output of the planner pipeline, followed by the runtime results for the full set of benchmark requests. To illustrate the route and trajectory-generation result produced by the Planning Service, we use the request with take-off vertiport \texttt{BLAN} and landing vertiport \texttt{WOHL}. The request uses the fixed cruise layer of \SI{200}{m} MSL and does not invoke the Deconfliction Service; deconfliction is treated as a separate service boundary in this architecture. This case was selected because it traverses multiple corridor segments and clearly separates departure, cruise, and arrival phases. For this origin--destination pair, the A* planner selects the corridor sequence
\[
\texttt{BLAN} \rightarrow \texttt{VOLK} \rightarrow \texttt{SCHN} \rightarrow
\texttt{FUHB} \rightarrow \texttt{WOHL}.
\]

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_paths_overview} shows this route in the context of the Hamburg corridor graph. The thinner blue edges represent available corridor connections, the red markers show graph nodes, and the thicker blue polyline highlights the selected A* path from \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL}. This overview makes the global routing result visible before the route is expanded into a detailed 4D trajectory.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_paths_overview.png}
  \caption{A* corridor path overview for the \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL} request. The highlighted path connects the departure vertiport \texttt{BLAN} with the landing vertiport \texttt{WOHL} through the selected corridor sequence.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_paths_overview}
\end{figure}

The waypoint synthesizer expands this five-node route into 12 CFMS input waypoints, including departure, transition, cruise, top-of-descent, and landing waypoints. The three internal cruise-node waypoints corresponding to \texttt{VOLK}, \texttt{SCHN}, and \texttt{FUHB} are annotated with the corridor-width-derived turn-radius parameter of \SI{20}{m}. The resulting dense trajectory contains 1892 samples before downstream subsampling, covers a cumulative distance of \SI{29.49}{km}, and has a total duration of \SI{1558.59}{s}, or \SI{25.98}{min}. The mean true airspeed over the generated trajectory is \SI{17.07}{m/s}, with the cruise segment reaching approximately \SI{25.00}{m/s}.

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_map} separates the lateral trajectory by the main planning phases. The departure panel shows the vehicle leaving \texttt{BLAN}, climbing through the terminal waypoints, and joining the corridor at \texttt{VOLK}. The cruise panel shows the route through the selected corridor nodes \texttt{VOLK}, \texttt{SCHN}, and \texttt{FUHB}; the regenerated trajectory uses the corridor-width turn-radius parameter at these cruise nodes. The landing panel shows the inbound transition from \texttt{FUHB} toward the destination vertiport \texttt{WOHL}. The figure therefore confirms that the generated trajectory follows the A*-selected corridor sequence while adding the terminal geometry required at both endpoints and finite-radius turn handling at internal cruise nodes. The corresponding vertical and speed profiles are shown in Figs.~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_altitude} and~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_tas}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_trajectory_map.png}
  \caption{Phase-separated lateral trajectory for the \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL} request after applying corridor-width turn-radius annotations at internal cruise nodes. Red markers indicate synthesized CFMS waypoints; the blue line is the generated trajectory. The panels separate the take-off and ascent portion, the corridor cruise portion, and the final landing transition. Boundary waypoints are repeated across adjacent panels to show phase continuity.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_map}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_altitude} shows the vertical profile of the generated trajectory. The three panels use local phase-relative time axes rather than one continuous global time axis, so each phase can be inspected at an appropriate temporal scale. During take-off and ascent, the trajectory climbs from the pad through the terminal heights and reaches the first corridor node below the cruise layer. The vehicle then reaches and maintains the \SI{200}{m} MSL cruise layer during the central corridor segment. On arrival, the trajectory holds the cruise layer until the inbound descent segment, then descends through the \SI{60}{m} and \SI{15}{m} terminal references before reaching the \texttt{WOHL} pad. This profile illustrates how the waypoint-synthesis logic combines the fixed cruise altitude with structured vertiport departure and arrival procedures.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.74\linewidth]{figures/blan_wohl_trajectory_altitude.png}
  \caption{Altitude profile for the \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL} trajectory. The three panels show take-off/ascent, cruise, and landing using local phase-relative time axes rather than a single global time axis. The profile reaches the fixed \SI{200}{m} MSL cruise layer before descending into \texttt{WOHL}.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_altitude}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:blan_wohl_tas} shows the corresponding true airspeed profile. As in the altitude profile, the panels use local phase-relative time axes rather than one global time axis. The vehicle accelerates from the terminal take-off segment into the outbound transition, reaches the higher cruise setting along the central corridor segment, and then decelerates for the arrival and landing procedure. The cruise panel also shows localized speed reductions at the internal corridor nodes. These reductions are expected: the regenerated waypoint set assigns a \SI{20}{m} turn radius to the cruise-node waypoints so that the vehicle can remain laterally inside the corridor while switching from one A*-selected corridor leg to the next. CFMS therefore reduces speed locally around these turns to satisfy the finite-radius transition constraint before accelerating again on the following corridor segment.

  \caption{True airspeed profile for the \texttt{BLAN} to \texttt{WOHL} trajectory. The phase-separated panels use local phase-relative time axes and show the acceleration from take-off, the higher cruise-speed segment, localized speed reductions for finite-radius transitions between adjacent A* corridor legs, and the deceleration into the landing procedure.}
  \label{fig:blan_wohl_tas}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Planner Computational Scaling Evaluation}
\label{sec:planner_scalability}

The computational scaling experiment evaluates the Planning Service in isolation from the Deconfliction Service. It measures a sequential baseline over a fixed batch of requests and scaling with selected path distance, not concurrent service throughput. A batch of 100 randomly sampled origin--destination requests was processed synchronously. Each request used the Hamburg corridor graph, the fixed \SI{200}{m} MSL cruise layer, and disabled deconfliction. The benchmark pairs were sampled without replacement, each pair had distinct origin and destination vertiports, and no origin--destination pair was repeated. All 100 sampled pairs were connected and all 100 requests completed successfully, so no failed cases were removed before computing the reported statistics.

Table~\ref{tab:planner_scalability_runtime} summarizes the measured runtime distribution for the planner-relevant stages. We use the term \emph{planner-stage subtotal} for the sum of A* path computation, waypoint synthesis, and CFMS invocation with trajectory generation. This subtotal has a mean of \SI{88.94}{ms} and a \(p_{90}\) latency of \SI{132.24}{ms}. This latency range is compatible with interactive planning and rapid route preview in the evaluated local setup, without implying operational readiness under distributed or concurrent traffic conditions. CFMS invocation and trajectory generation dominate this subtotal with a mean of \SI{88.27}{ms}. In contrast, waypoint synthesis and A* path search remain sub-millisecond, with \(p_{99}\) latencies of \SI{0.41}{ms} and \SI{0.83}{ms}, respectively.

CFMS invocation and trajectory generation account for almost the entire planner-stage runtime, while A* and waypoint synthesis are negligible in comparison. Using the mean values in Table~\ref{tab:planner_scalability_runtime}, CFMS invocation and trajectory generation contribute approximately \(88.27/88.94 \approx 99.2\%\) of the planner-stage subtotal. This makes CFMS invocation and trajectory generation the dominant computational stage in the evaluated planner pipeline. Because this stage dominates the subtotal, the distance dependence of the subtotal is expected to follow the distance dependence of the CFMS-related generation stage.

Figure~\ref{fig:planner_scalability_distance} relates the planner-stage runtimes to selected path distance. The planner-stage subtotal and CFMS invocation with trajectory generation both increase strongly with distance (Pearson \(r=0.90\)), showing that route length primarily affects the downstream trajectory-generation workload. The likely reason is that longer corridor routes produce longer dense trajectories, more trajectory samples, and therefore larger CFMS generation workloads. Waypoint synthesis (Pearson \(r=-0.06\)) and A* path search (Pearson \(r=0.11\)) show little distance sensitivity and remain small in absolute terms. This indicates that, for the evaluated network, route search is not the limiting factor.

Planner-stage subtotal latency and CFMS invocation with trajectory-generation latency both increase with distance bucket and show wider spread for longer paths. A* latency remains tightly bounded across all distance buckets, which is the main strength of the planner architecture in the evaluated scenario: graph search has negligible latency compared with trajectory generation. The corresponding histograms in Fig.~\ref{fig:planner_scalability_hist} show that the planner-stage subtotal distribution is effectively the trajectory-generation distribution with a small sub-millisecond contribution from waypoint synthesis and graph search.
