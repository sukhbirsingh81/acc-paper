\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Urban Air Mobility (UAM) is emerging as a promising extension of future transportation systems. To enable the safe integration of large numbers of electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) vehicles into low-altitude airspace, structured traffic-management frameworks such as the European U-space~\cite{eu_commission_2017,easa2020uspace} and the U.S. UTM concept~\cite{kopardekar2016utm} have been proposed. These initiatives emphasize service-oriented architectures and trajectory-based operations to support scalable and safe airspace integration~\cite{sesar_conops_2019,sesar_master_plan_2025}.

A central requirement in such environments is computationally efficient path planning. In dense urban scenarios, trajectory requests must be processed while respecting separation requirements and structured corridor constraints. Search-based approaches such as A* have been widely investigated for UAV routing~\cite{sahawneh2016_3d_path_planning,a_star_2d3d_comparison_utm}, alongside 4D grid-based conflict management strategies~\cite{acevedo_4d_grids_u_space}. Deconfliction in multi-dimensional trajectory spaces has also been studied in trajectory-based operations frameworks~\cite{NDMap,enea_porretta_4d_tbo_comparison}, and surveys summarize the broader range of UAV path-planning methods~\cite{ahmad2023_uav_path_planning_survey}.

In operational U-space systems, path planning is not an isolated routing problem. A selected route must be converted into a time-parameterized 4D trajectory, checked against operational constraints, and integrated into a service-oriented traffic-management workflow. Monolithic implementations of these functions may limit extensibility and independent scaling. Microservice architectures offer a modular alternative by separating concerns such as topology provision, trajectory generation, deconfliction, and authorization into dedicated services~\cite{dragoni2017microservices,matlekovic2020uav}. However, quantitative evaluations of service-oriented U-space planning systems remain limited, particularly with respect to latency decomposition and computational scaling with path distance.

This paper presents a service-oriented 4D path-planning architecture for structured U-space corridor operations and evaluates the baseline computational performance of its planner stage. The architecture separates graph-based route planning, waypoint synthesis, 4D trajectory generation, and deconfliction into distinct service responsibilities. The planner stage combines A*-based routing on a vertiport corridor graph with CFMS-based trajectory generation~\cite{morscheck2021cfms}. A CesiumJS-based frontend enables interactive visualization of the generated time-dependent trajectories~\cite{cesiumjs_software}.

The evaluated planning pipeline follows four main steps. First, A* selects a shortest corridor route on the vertiport graph. Second, waypoint synthesis augments this route with departure, climb, cruise, descent, and landing structure. Third, CFMS converts the structured waypoint sequence into a dense time-parameterized 4D trajectory. Finally, the runtime analysis decomposes the planner stage to identify the dominant computational cost.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A service-oriented U-space path-planning architecture that separates topology provision, route planning, trajectory generation, and deconfliction responsibilities.
    \item A CFMS-compatible waypoint-synthesis pipeline that converts A*-selected corridor routes into phase-structured inputs for 4D trajectory generation.
    \item A planner-stage latency decomposition into A* path computation, waypoint synthesis, and CFMS invocation with trajectory generation.
    \item A Hamburg vertiport corridor-network evaluation using sequential trajectory requests to characterize planner-stage computational scaling with selected path distance.
\end{itemize}
