\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{U-Space and 4D Trajectory-Based Operations}

The SESAR Master Plan situates U-Space within the broader transition toward trajectory-based operations (TBO) and digitally enabled air traffic management~\cite{sesar_master_plan_2025}. Comparable developments in FAA NextGen emphasize negotiated 4D trajectories to improve predictability and capacity~\cite{enea_porretta_4d_tbo_comparison}. These initiatives highlight time-constrained trajectory management and interoperable services as key enablers of scalable urban air traffic integration.

Conflict management in discretized 4D space–time representations has been demonstrated for multi-UAV operations~\cite{acevedo_4d_grids_u_space}. Furthermore, architectural concepts for U-Space services propose modular stacks integrating monitoring and traffic management functions~\cite{uspace_inflight_services_architecture}. While these works provide important conceptual foundations, empirical performance evaluations of deployable end-to-end systems remain limited.

\subsection{UAV Path Planning and Conflict Detection}

Search-based planners, particularly A*-based methods, remain widely used for low-altitude UAV routing due to their deterministic behavior and manageable computational complexity~\cite{sahawneh2016_3d_path_planning,a_star_2d3d_comparison_utm}. Studies comparing 2D and 3D search highlight trade-offs between runtime and path optimality, with higher-dimensional search increasing computational cost.

Conflict detection in multi-dimensional trajectory spaces has been extensively studied within trajectory-based operations frameworks~\cite{NDMap,enea_porretta_4d_tbo_comparison}. These approaches formalize separation assessment in time-dependent state spaces and provide a foundation for integrated 4D conflict management.

Although algorithmic aspects of routing and conflict detection are well established, many studies evaluate these components in isolation rather than as parts of distributed, service-oriented architectures operating under traffic load.

\subsection{Microservice Architectures in UAV Systems}

Microservice architectures have gained prominence in distributed software engineering due to their modularity and independent scalability~\cite{dragoni2017microservices}. Applications in UAV-related domains demonstrate benefits in system maintainability and flexible deployment~\cite{matlekovic2020uav}. 

However, the integration of search-based routing, 4D trajectory synthesis, and conflict detection within a unified microservice-based U-Space stack has received comparatively limited quantitative evaluation, particularly with respect to latency behaviour and scalability under increasing demand.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Research Gap.}
Existing literature establishes theoretical and architectural foundations for U-space integration and trajectory planning. However, few works report end-to-end latency measurements of a deployable path planning stack that integrates routing, trajectory generation, and conflict checking. In particular, runtime breakdowns separating graph-based routing from physically feasible trajectory synthesis are rarely quantified. This paper addresses that gap by evaluating the computational performance of a full-stack implementation under a realistic vertiport network scenario.
