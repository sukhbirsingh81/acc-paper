\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{U-Space and 4D Trajectory-Based Operations}

The SESAR Master Plan situates U-Space within the broader transition toward trajectory-based operations (TBO) and digitally enabled air traffic management~\cite{sesar_master_plan_2025}. Comparable developments in FAA NextGen emphasize negotiated 4D trajectories to improve predictability and capacity~\cite{enea_porretta_4d_tbo_comparison}. These initiatives highlight time-constrained trajectory management and interoperable services as key enablers of scalable urban air traffic integration.

Conflict management in discretized 4D space–time representations has been demonstrated for multi-UAV operations~\cite{acevedo_4d_grids_u_space}. Furthermore, architectural concepts for U-Space services propose modular stacks integrating monitoring and traffic management functions~\cite{uspace_inflight_services_architecture}. While these works provide important conceptual foundations, empirical performance evaluations of deployable end-to-end systems remain limited.

\subsection{UAV Path Planning and Conflict Detection}

Search-based planners remain widely used for UAV routing in structured airspace. In corridor-based airspace models, the routing problem can be represented as a sparse graph where nodes correspond to vertiports and edges represent corridor connections. Classical shortest-path algorithms such as Dijkstra’s algorithm guarantee optimal solutions but explore the graph uniformly without considering the direction of the destination.

Heuristic search methods such as A* reduce this exploration by guiding the search toward the goal using an admissible heuristic. In corridor networks, the straight-line geodesic distance to the destination provides an efficient heuristic that significantly reduces the number of expanded nodes while preserving optimality~\cite{sahawneh2016_3d_path_planning,a_star_2d3d_comparison_utm}. For this reason, A*-based planners are widely used for UAV routing in structured airspace.

Conflict detection and resolution in multi-dimensional trajectory spaces have been extensively studied within trajectory-based operations frameworks~\cite{NDMap,enea_porretta_4d_tbo_comparison}. These approaches formalize separation assessment in time-dependent state spaces and provide a foundation for integrated 4D conflict management.

Although algorithmic aspects of routing and deconfliction are well established, many studies evaluate these components in isolation rather than as parts of distributed, service-oriented architectures operating under traffic load.

\subsection{Microservice Architectures in UAV Systems}

Microservice architectures have gained prominence in distributed software engineering due to their modularity and independent scalability~\cite{dragoni2017microservices}. Applications in UAV-related domains demonstrate benefits in system maintainability and flexible deployment~\cite{matlekovic2020uav}. 

However, the integration of search-based routing, 4D trajectory synthesis, and deconfliction within a unified microservice-based U-Space stack has received comparatively limited quantitative evaluation, particularly with respect to latency behaviour and scalability under increasing demand.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Research Gap.}
Existing literature establishes theoretical and architectural foundations for U-space integration and trajectory planning. However, few works report end-to-end latency measurements of a deployable path planning stack that separates routing and trajectory generation from conflict detection and resolution. In particular, runtime breakdowns separating graph-based routing from physically feasible trajectory synthesis are rarely quantified. This paper addresses that gap by evaluating the computational performance of a full-stack implementation under a realistic vertiport network scenario while preserving a dedicated service boundary for deconfliction.
